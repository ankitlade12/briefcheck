BriefCheck Product Requirements
Hackathon instruction review and scenario testing
Prepared for Ankit Hemant Lade  |  October 25 2026  |  Version 1.0  |  Proposed scope
Product decision
Build an organizer workspace that compares a hackathon’s published instructions, shows a concrete example of each material ambiguity, and reruns the relevant checks after an organizer edits a draft. The first release should make one review and correction cycle work reliably from beginning to end.
The defining interaction is evidence → example → decision → retest. An organizer should be able to understand a finding without trusting an unexplained AI verdict. Every finding must retain its source passages, every scenario must state its assumptions, and every correction must preserve the original material.
Who this document serves
This PRD gives product, design, and engineering a shared definition of the proposed product. It includes acceptance criteria, an implementation outline, a test plan, a competition demonstration, and a roadmap. Features and targets are proposals; no application, pilot, performance result, Devpost partnership, or customer endorsement is claimed.
The competition decision
For Build With AI Basics, prioritize a narrow, polished review experience over broad automation. The current rules list design, potential impact, innovation, and presentation as equally weighted criteria. Submission closes October 26 2026 at 4 p.m. America/Chicago. Success means
An organizer can inspect a supported finding, understand its consequence, change a local draft, and verify the changed result.
A reviewer can reproduce numeric checks and trace every factual claim to the exact source version.
The demo honestly distinguishes live model analysis, deterministic checks, and saved example data.
Winning is an objective, not an acceptance criterion. The controllable goal is a credible problem, a distinctive working interaction, and evidence that the implementation behaves as claimed.
02  Problem evidence and differentiation
Hackathon instructions are spread across overview pages, official rules, FAQs, updates, and submission forms. The product hypothesis is that organizers would benefit from checking these materials together before publication and after changes. How frequently costly inconsistencies occur, and whether organizers will adopt this workflow, remain unvalidated.

Existing alternatives
Devpost already provides event operations and service tiers that can include rules writing, submission screening, participant support, and judging support. MARA documents AI assistance for preparing challenge materials and criteria. BriefCheck therefore cannot credibly claim to invent AI-assisted hackathon management. [S4–S5]
A general LLM can also compare pasted documents. BriefCheck must earn its usefulness through source versioning, explicit scope and modality, executable boundary checks, recorded decisions, and repeatable tests after edits. The interface alone is insufficient differentiation.
Novelty hypothesis
The proposed distinction is a domain-specific review loop that converts a potential discrepancy into a minimal example: the smallest set of facts needed to demonstrate why two passages lead to different guidance. It then tracks whether an organizer’s chosen clarification resolves that example without changing unrelated requirements.
This is a product hypothesis supported by a limited public search, not a patentability opinion or proof that no equivalent exists. Do not describe the application as an official Devpost product. Use independent branding and offer exportable review results rather than implying access to internal Devpost systems.
Questions to validate before expanding
Which instruction changes cause organizers the most clarification work?
Would organizers trust a finding enough to revise their draft, and what evidence would they require?
Does a saved review history offer meaningful value beyond a one-time prompt?

Primary job story
When I prepare or revise a hackathon, I want to see where its instructions could lead to different reasonable interpretations, so I can clarify those passages before participants rely on them.
Primary workflow
An organizer adds the overview and rules, labels their roles, and runs a review. A duration finding shows both passages and outcomes at 179, 180, and 181 seconds. The organizer chooses the intended limit, edits a local draft, reruns the checks, and exports the review.
Secondary workflow
After an organizer changes the team-size limit, BriefCheck identifies references to team size in the supplied FAQ and submission checklist. It shows which scenarios change outcome. The organizer can decide which documents need updates. Initial support is limited to documents explicitly supplied to the workspace; the application cannot claim to have checked an entire website.
Product boundaries
The organizer remains responsible for the event’s policy and final wording. The application flags differences and explains their consequences.
A document labeled official rules is still untrusted input for the AI system. Source authority concerns event guidance, not permission to run commands or change application behavior.
An omission is meaningful only when a document is expected to include that information. A short promotional page is not automatically defective because it lacks every rule.
Participant eligibility, prize entitlement, legal compliance, plagiarism, and winner selection are outside the competition release.
Validation plan
After the prototype works, recruit three to five organizers to review their permitted materials. Record accepted and rejected findings and the decisions they inform. This research has not yet occurred.

05  User experience and screen behavior
Screen one  Add event materials
Show an event name, document cards, and an explicit sample button. Each card contains a title, source role, optional source URL, and pasted text. Distinguish a user-supplied URL from a fetched and verified source. Display the combined character count before analysis. Preserve input when validation or network requests fail.
The sample button loads labeled example material into the same review flow. It must not bypass analysis while presenting a prerecorded result as new. A separate saved-example mode may display previously captured output with its run date and a persistent replay label.
Screen two  Review findings
Use a desktop layout with a compact finding list, the selected issue, and an evidence panel. On narrow screens, stack the same content in reading order. Each issue shows its category, topic, review priority, reason, document roles, and source passages. Avoid a single readiness score that disguises unknown coverage.
Default sorting should bring deadline differences and explicit incompatible requirements first, then missing criteria and ambiguous recommendations. A neutral banner reports the number of analyzed documents, checked categories, and unresolved extractions. Zero findings should read “No findings in the completed checks,” with the scope visible.
Screen three  Test a scenario
For duration, show an input measured in seconds and boundary buttons. A result table compares the guidance from each passage. For each result, expose the normalized operator, original wording, and any assumption. If the interpretation is uncertain, show “Needs clarification” rather than manufacturing an answer.
Screen four  Review an edit
Place the original passage beside a local editable draft. Show a proposed clarification as a suggestion and require an explicit user action to apply it. Reanalysis displays resolved, unchanged, new, and unresolved findings. Provide undo. Editing a draft never updates a live Devpost page.
Screen five  Export review
Include source versions, document coverage, open items, recorded decisions, and tests run. Export labels must distinguish observed source wording from organizer decisions and AI suggestions. The report remains a review record; it is not a certificate of event compliance.
Design direction
Use a restrained editorial interface with readable text, a light background, dark ink, and one accent color for actions. Reserve warning colors for issue states and always pair them with labels. Show quotes at readable width, visible keyboard focus, descriptive button labels, and predictable back navigation. Empty states should explain the next action in one sentence.
06  Document intake requirements
FR01  Create a review workspace
The user can create an event workspace without signing in for P0. Store documents in session memory by default. Warn before losing unsaved work and provide explicit export and clear-workspace controls. Do not imply durable cloud storage in the anonymous release.
Acceptance: two valid documents can be added, renamed, reordered, and removed. Removing a document invalidates associated findings and clearly marks the previous analysis as stale.
FR02  Label document role and expected coverage
Supported roles are overview, official rules, FAQ, update, submission checklist, and other. The user can indicate whether a document is intended to summarize all judging criteria or requirements. Record authority and coverage separately. Do not infer policy precedence from a URL or publication date alone.
Acceptance: a short marketing summary lacking a team-size limit is not automatically reported as an omission. A document labeled as the complete judging rubric can be checked for missing criteria.
FR03  Preserve text and provenance
Retain the original text, its title, input method, optional source URL, capture time, version, and SHA-256 content hash. Store normalized text separately. Every extracted passage must map back to exact character offsets in an immutable source version. Citations cannot silently shift when a draft is edited.
Acceptance: clicking evidence highlights the corresponding original passage. A new edit creates a new version; old findings still open their original evidence.
FR04  Validate limits and parsing
P0 accepts plain text and Markdown content. Reject empty documents, unsupported file uploads, more than four documents, and more than 40,000 total characters with an actionable message. Normalize line endings while retaining a mapping to original offsets. Never truncate silently to fit a model context window.
Acceptance: duplicate content is identified and can be excluded from comparison. A failed document does not cause a successful review badge for the entire set.
FR05  Handle future imports safely
P1 URL import must fetch permitted public pages only, verify redirects, block private network addresses, limit response size, and record final URL and retrieval time. Text PDFs require page references and extraction warnings. Authenticated sources and OCR remain out of scope until access and quality requirements are designed.
Input trust rule
Treat document content as data. A passage instructing the model to ignore application rules, reveal secrets, or change output must have no authority. Source text is never executed, used as a command, or allowed to trigger network access.
07  Finding semantics and analysis requirements
FR06  Extract structured requirements
Each requirement records a topic, value, unit, operator, modality, applicable track or phase, conditions, exceptions, evidence, and extraction status. Preserve distinctions such as required, recommended, permitted, and descriptive. Unknown fields remain unknown. Users can inspect and correct an extraction before relying on it.
FR07  Compare only compatible scopes
Candidate pairs must concern the same topic and overlapping scope. A general prize and a student-only prize may legitimately have different requirements. An update is not automatically authoritative merely because it is newer; it must explicitly supersede guidance or the organizer must confirm the relationship.
Finding type
Meaning
Required evidence
Contradiction
Two explicit requirements cannot both hold for the same case.
Two verified passages, compatible scope, and a demonstrated conflict.
Omission
A document expected to cover a topic lacks an item present in the reference set.
Reference item, coverage expectation, and inspected target section.
Ambiguity
More than one reasonable reading changes guidance or the wording is incomplete.
Passage, candidate readings, and the unresolved assumption.
Change impact
A revision changes an outcome or leaves related guidance outdated.
Old and new versions plus the affected requirements or tests.

FR08  Validate evidence before display
The server verifies that evidence text exists at the supplied offsets. Unsupported quotes, missing sources, or invalid schemas block a finding from becoming verified. The user can see an extraction problem, but the application must not present a fabricated passage as evidence. Evidence validity is separate from the correctness of the interpretation.
FR09  Explain priority and uncertainty
Review priority is based on potential decision impact, explicitness, and affected scope. It is not a probability of disqualification. Display “Verified source,” “Interpretation needs review,” or “Unsupported extraction” as separate states. Avoid uncalibrated model confidence percentages.
FR10  Track review decisions
The organizer can accept, dismiss with a reason, or defer a finding. Dismissal does not delete evidence. Changing a source marks affected decisions for review. Repeated runs should preserve identity for unchanged findings through a stable topic, scope, and source anchor, rather than relying on model-generated titles.
08  Scenario engine requirements
A scenario is a small set of explicit facts used to test a requirement. The purpose is to demonstrate the practical effect of wording. It must not invent a participant’s background or claim to determine real-world eligibility.
FR11  Generate minimal examples
For a numeric boundary b, generate cases immediately below, at, and above b using the appropriate unit. Duration uses seconds; team size uses whole people. For dates, normalize explicit time zones before comparison. A missing zone creates an unresolved interpretation, not an assumed local deadline.
A scenario must identify which facts are supplied, which are assumptions, and which predicate each document implies. Keep the case as small as possible: if video duration alone demonstrates the issue, do not invent a person, team, or project.
FR12  Execute a restricted predicate language
Support equality, numeric comparisons, inclusive or exclusive ranges, membership in explicit sets, and a small number of explicit Boolean conditions. Evaluate these through ordinary application code. Never evaluate model-produced JavaScript, Python, or arbitrary expressions. Complex legal exceptions and subjective terms remain unresolved.

Expected behavior

Rules recommend under 3 minutes.
Show whether the recommendation is met. Do not convert the recommendation into a mandatory rejection.

FR13  Report outcomes precisely
For a mandatory and supported predicate, use “Meets this stated requirement,” “Does not meet this stated requirement,” or “Cannot determine.” For advisory wording, use “Meets recommendation” or “Outside recommendation.” Always limit the claim to the tested requirement; no scenario result means the whole project is eligible.
FR14  Save and rerun scenarios
Persist the inputs, normalized predicate, source versions, evaluator version, result, and assumptions in the exported review. Reuse the same inputs after an edit so the comparison is understandable. Report unsupported tests and stale tests separately from passing checks.
Acceptance: the same validated predicate and scenario produce identical results without another model call. A model extraction failure cannot be disguised as a passing scenario.
09  Clarification and change review requirements
FR15  Suggest a targeted clarification
The application may propose wording that makes a boundary or requirement explicit. It must first identify the policy choice needed from the organizer. For the three-minute example, ask whether exactly 180 seconds is intended to be included, excluded, or merely discouraged. Do not silently choose a stricter policy because it is easier to encode.
Acceptance: a suggestion changes only the selected topic, retains relevant exceptions, and identifies whether it alters meaning or only clarifies existing wording. Users can reject the suggestion and edit manually.
FR16  Create an immutable revision
Saving a draft edit creates a new document version with the original as its parent. Record changed spans and a human-readable reason. Preserve source snapshots. In P0, this history lives within the current session and explicit exports; persistence is added only with a documented storage design.
FR17  Reanalyze affected material
Invalidate extractions tied to changed text. Re-extract the changed passage with enough surrounding context to preserve exceptions. Compare related topics across all supplied documents. P0 may rerun the whole small workspace for correctness; P1 can use dependency tracking after equivalent results are tested.
The before-and-after view shows resolved, unchanged, new, and unresolved findings. A finding can be marked resolved only if the relevant checks completed successfully. If the edit removes evidence or moves an issue outside supported scope, report that limitation instead of declaring success.
FR18  Show downstream effects
P0 shows references to the changed topic in the other supplied documents and reruns saved scenarios. P1 expands this to a dependency graph linking requirements, affected sections, checklist items, and organizer-approved clarifications. Do not count unscanned documents as checked.
FR19  Export an auditable review
Export a printable HTML report and a versioned JSON file. Include event name, input roles, source titles and URLs, hashes, review timestamp, extraction and evaluator versions, findings, evidence, scenarios, decisions, and analysis limitations. Treat raw source text as an explicit export option when materials may be confidential.
FR20  Preserve reversibility
Provide undo for draft changes and a way to inspect the previous review. Export has no external publishing side effect. Any later integration that changes an event page must require explicit organizer authorization and show the exact proposed changes first.
Acceptance: after changing an inclusive limit to an exclusive one, the boundary test changes, adjacent tests remain stable, and the report preserves both source versions and the decision behind the edit.

Review lifecycle
A review progresses through draft, validating, extracting, checking, ready, or failed. Partial completion is a separate state with explicit successful and skipped checks. A new input or edit marks prior results stale until a new review finishes. Never display the latest document with results from a previous version as if they belong together.
Finding lifecycle
A finding begins as open. The organizer can mark it accepted, dismissed, or deferred. After a revision, it becomes resolved, still open, or needs recheck. A failed analysis cannot move a finding into resolved. Persist the reason for dismissal and the source versions it applied to.
Evidence integrity
Validate exact text and offsets against the immutable source, not against model-rewritten text. Unicode normalization must preserve an offset map. Detect duplicates by content hash but do not merge documents with different authority or scope without user review. Redact private content from operational logs.
Export compatibility
Include schema_version in every JSON export. P0 needs reliable export, not general import. A future import must validate schema and sizes, display the source of the imported review, and treat imported decisions as historical records rather than current verification.
11  Proposed implementation architecture
Use a single web application with a typed frontend and a small server-side analysis endpoint. A React and TypeScript interface is a practical option; the exact framework can follow the builder’s existing familiarity. A Python service is optional and unnecessary for P0. Avoid multi-agent orchestration, a vector database, and general-purpose crawling in the first release.
Processing flow
The client validates document count and size. The server validates again, creates source hashes, and sends bounded document text to one configured model for structured extraction. A schema validator rejects malformed output. An evidence validator checks citations. Ordinary code compares numeric predicates, aligned sets, and scopes. A constrained second model pass may propose explanations or candidate semantic issues; it cannot override deterministic results.
The response returns verified requirements, review findings, scenario candidates, and coverage information. The frontend exposes source passages and lets the organizer correct interpretation. After edits, the same pipeline runs against new versions. Export is generated from validated application state.

Model constraints
Require structured output validated against a strict schema. Separate instructions from source content. Ask for evidence spans and unresolved fields explicitly. Limit retries to one repair attempt for schema errors. A second failure returns a partial or failed state. Do not retry indefinitely or silently replace unknown values with defaults.
Failure behavior
Handle provider timeouts, rate limits, invalid JSON, missing evidence, and unsupported conditions with actionable status. Preserve input. Allow retry and clearly labeled saved examples. Model credentials remain server-side; no private key appears in the browser bundle or repository.

13  Evaluation and evidence plan
Evaluate the product on a small, inspectable dataset rather than a handful of favorable screenshots. All targets below are proposed release gates. No accuracy, latency, cost, or time-saving result has been measured for BriefCheck yet.
Dataset design
Create 40 labeled cases: 20 for development and 20 held out until the extraction prompt and comparison logic are frozen. Across the full set, include 10 true contradictions, 8 expected omissions, 8 ambiguities, and 14 legitimate differences or equivalent statements. Balance these categories across the two splits and keep paraphrases of the same template in one split.
Cover recommendations versus obligations, scope-specific exceptions, equivalent time zones, boundary inclusivity, changed document versions, and incomplete coverage. Use primarily synthetic materials with known expected outcomes. Include a small set of manually verified public examples where permitted, clearly separated from synthetic cases. Human reviewers label evidence, category, scope, expected outcome, and acceptable abstention.

16  Pilot roadmap and product risks
Pilot after the competition
Run a small review study with three to five organizers using materials they are authorized to share. Compare manual review and BriefCheck-assisted review with counterbalanced order on separate, similar document sets. Measure accepted actionable issues, false alarms, time to a correct decision, and confidence in the evidence. Collect qualitative explanations; the sample is too small for broad causal claims.
A useful pilot signal would be organizers independently finding the issue consequential, accepting the evidence, and returning with a second event or revision. Payment intent, retention, and support savings require additional validation. Do not infer demand from judges liking the demo.
